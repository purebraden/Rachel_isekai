# Chapter 10 -- Notice

Sivara did not open the paired volume immediately.

It remained where she had left it the night before, resting flat beneath
the warded window where early light gathered without glare, and for
several measured breaths she stood beside it without touching the cover,
aware of the faint current beneath her composure that had nothing to do
with fear and everything to do with recognition.

When she did open it, she did so with the same economy of motion she
brought to all deliberate acts. The ink darkened beneath her pen as she
wrote, the words forming with controlled precision rather than formal
declaration: structural variance observed; foundational alignment
documentation incomplete; responsive instability confirmed; walker
designation unresolved. She did not embellish. She did not speculate.
She recorded.

The waiting was shorter than she had expected.

The surface of the page shimmered faintly before new script emerged
opposite her own, the strokes unmistakably Council hand and unadorned in
tone. Emissary dispatched. Shadow route. Arrival estimated one day.

For a fraction of a breath, something flickered across her expression
--- not surprise exactly, and not alarm, but a tightening that
acknowledged the speed of response and what that speed implied. Then it
passed, replaced by stillness that was not calm so much as
recalculation. The Council did not move quickly without reason. If they
had chosen shadow passage rather than formal delegation, it meant the
matter was not procedural.

She closed the volume and rested her palm briefly against the cover, as
if feeling for residual warmth, before stepping into the yard where
Rachel was already waiting.

Morning had sharpened since the earlier grey. The air held a cleaner
edge, the kind that made sound carry farther than expected. Rachel stood
near the ward line, not pacing but alert in a way that suggested she had
sensed the shift in Sivara's focus before it was spoken aloud.

"You wanted to continue from yesterday," Rachel said, not quite a
question.

"Yes," Sivara replied, studying her with a gaze that did not yet reveal
what it measured. "Show me how you corrected the micro fractures along
the eastern road."

Rachel hesitated only long enough to orient herself, then extended her
awareness toward the yard's outer warding, where faint stress always
gathered in the morning as the seam adjusted to movement beyond
Thornhaven's boundaries. The warmth in her palms rose almost
immediately, cleaner and more direct than she remembered it being months
ago, and instead of pushing against resistance she felt something closer
to alignment, as though the instability recognized the shape she offered
it and settled into place without argument.

The correction was not dramatic. It did not flare or pulse. It simply
held.

Sivara did not nod.

"Again," she said.

Rachel withdrew and reached once more, this time noticing the absence of
strain where she expected it. The warmth moved through her more quickly,
spreading from palm to wrist without the lag that had once accompanied
every adjustment, and the seam's tension eased with minimal insistence
on her part. It felt less like bracing a door against wind and more like
guiding something into a frame that had been waiting.

When she lowered her hands, she became aware that Sivara was watching
her differently.

Not with approval. Not with doubt.

With focus sharpened past instruction.

"You are not compensating," Sivara said at length.

Rachel frowned, the phrasing catching her attention before its meaning
did. "That's what boundary walkers do."

"Yes," Sivara agreed, and the agreement carried more weight than
contradiction might have. "They compensate for instability. They counter
tension with force proportional to the strain."

Rachel looked down at her hands as if they might offer clarification.
"Then what am I doing?"

Sivara's gaze shifted briefly toward the ward line before returning to
Rachel. For an instant it seemed she might answer directly, and Rachel
felt a flicker of anticipation that had nothing to do with pride and
everything to do with needing a name for what was happening inside her.

Instead, Sivara said, "Again."

Rachel obeyed.

The third correction came more easily still, not because she exerted
more effort but because she required less. The instability adjusted
before she fully extended toward it, and she felt the change ripple
through her awareness with a subtle intimacy that unsettled her more
than resistance ever had.

When she lowered her hands this time, she did not ask for evaluation.
She waited.

Sivara did not offer praise. She studied the seam as if recalibrating
doctrine against observation, and Rachel felt the distance between
teacher and subject narrow into something more clinical.

"You feel it differently," Sivara said at last.

Rachel hesitated, aware of how much truth sat behind that simple
statement. "It's faster," she admitted. "It doesn't push back the way it
used to."

"And you do not push against it," Sivara replied quietly.

Rachel looked up. "Should I?"

The question hung between them, not accusatory but earnest.

Sivara did not answer immediately, and in that pause Rachel became
acutely aware that whatever had shifted was not confined to her own
body. It existed in the space between them as well, in the way Sivara's
attention had narrowed and steadied.

"There will be an emissary," Sivara said finally, as though the two
subjects were not separate.

Rachel absorbed the statement without reacting at once. "From the
Council?"

"Yes."

"Three weeks?"

"Shadow route," Sivara corrected. "Arrival tomorrow."

The air seemed to thin slightly at that, though no visible change marked
it. Rachel did not feel threatened so much as observed in advance, as if
something were already orienting toward her.

"Is that unusual?" she asked.

"For routine matters," Sivara said, and left the remainder unsaid.

Rachel nodded slowly, then turned her attention back toward the ward
line, where a faint disturbance flickered along the outer edge, subtle
enough that another walker might have ignored it as ambient fluctuation.
She did not step forward. She extended her awareness instead, allowing
the warmth to rise and shape itself before she consciously directed it,
and the disturbance stilled with minimal resistance.

She felt Sivara watching.

Not assessing performance.

Assessing pattern.

The seam hummed beneath the yard in a register she had begun to
recognize as baseline, and yet it felt closer now, less distant than it
had in earlier months when every correction required strain. She did not
know whether that proximity was cause for comfort or concern. She only
knew that when she withdrew her hands, the warmth lingered a moment
longer than it once had.

"Tomorrow," she repeated softly, more to herself than to Sivara.

"Yes," Sivara replied.

Neither of them moved immediately. The yard held its quiet. Beyond the
wards, Thornhaven carried on without visible disturbance, unaware that
attention had already begun to narrow toward it.

Rachel flexed her fingers once, feeling the residual heat dissipate, and
wondered---not with fear, but with growing awareness---whether what she
was becoming had ever belonged to hinge doctrine at all.

The seam answered only with its steady hum.
