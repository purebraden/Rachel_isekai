# Chapter 15 — Imitation

The disturbance reaches her somewhere between the northern terraces and the orchard, where the boundary dips shallowly before rising again toward the fence. The afternoon stretches thin under heat. Insects work through the grass. Someone laughs near the well and the sound breaks apart against the stone of the square. The markers along the path hum at their usual pitch, low enough that most people forget they’re listening.

Something in that hum tightens.

Rachel slows without deciding to, palm resting against the nearest stone. It feels steady, cool from shadow, familiar against her skin. Then the strain shifts again from farther east, along the secondary line that threads between the orchard trees. The tension draws inward there, gathering too closely, pulling at a single point instead of traveling through.

No one nearby reacts. Two residents cross the lower path with baskets hooked over their arms, talking about the late pears. The wind lifts the upper branches and lets them settle.

The compression comes again.

Rachel turns toward it.

The orchard thins near the outer fence, trees spaced irregularly where the boundary widens before reconnecting. Caelen stands there in the uneven shade, boots planted carefully in the packed earth between two markers. He has chosen ground that rarely draws attention, a line that asks for reinforcement more often than most.

His hand hovers above the stone.

His wrist turns.

The first marker vibrates, a sharp tremor that catches instead of flowing. The motion travels to the next stone and hesitates, then strikes the third harder than it should. The strain collects around him, folding inward. The air between the trunks holds still, as if waiting to see where the tension will go.

He shifts his stance and tries again.

The gathering deepens.

Rachel moves before she decides to move. Leaves crack under her boots. A branch brushes her sleeve.

The pattern changes as she steps closer. The tightness around Caelen loosens in increments, spreading outward into the soil beneath the roots. The vibration along the markers evens gradually, settling into the familiar low hum that has come to feel like background breath.

Caelen’s hand drops.

He turns, color rising along his cheekbones.

“I just wanted to see,” he says.

Rachel keeps her eyes on the line, watching the place where the strain had drawn too close to itself.

“It isn’t the same.”

“It worked for you.” He says it carefully, like a statement he has rehearsed.

“That wasn’t the reason.”

“Then what was?”

She searches for something she can separate from herself — a sequence, a method, something that can be handed over. The memory of the hinge returns: the turn of her wrist, the sense of motion already underway, the way the strain widened instead of gathering.

“I don’t know.”

The orchard feels quieter around the admission.

“You don’t know.”

“It didn’t feel like I made it move,” she says. “It felt like it was already moving.”

“So alignment.”

“Maybe."

"Then teach me."

She lets out a breath that doesn’t quite become a laugh.

"I can’t teach something I don’t understand."

The markers hum evenly now, though she can still trace where the strain had pressed too hard against the line.

"You were trying to make it move," she says.

"And you weren’t?"

The wind slips between the trees and rattles the fence wire.

"I wasn’t trying to make it do anything," she says after a moment. "That might be the difference."

He absorbs that without agreement.

"If you try again," she says, "don’t do it alone."

He nods once, shoulders drawn tight, and steps back from the line. The orchard swallows the sound of his retreating boots.

---

At the edge of the slope, Vaelis stands where the ground drops just enough to give him sight of the secondary line without placing him inside it. Late light threads through the leaves and shifts across the page in his hand. He had marked the first irregular pulse before Rachel turned toward it — a compression traveling without redistribution.

He watches Caelen’s wrist turn.

The first marker vibrates. The second lags. The third answers with more force than the line comfortably holds. The strain gathers around the boy’s position and resists dispersal.

He lowers his eyes to the page.

Applied vector without structural accommodation.

The ink dries slowly in the heat. He adds another line beneath it.

Localized compression observed along secondary line. Pattern inconsistent with subject R.M.

Rachel enters the line without touching it.

The vibration alters.

The gathered tension loosens in shallow increments, traveling outward through soil and root and stone in a way that suggests redistribution rather than counterpressure. The shift begins before contact.

He underlines a phrase and writes again.

Response initiates at proximity threshold.

Her voice carries faintly across the orchard.

"I don’t know."

His pen pauses before moving again.

Subject unable to articulate initiating mechanism.

Replication risk increases under visibility conditions.

The breeze lifts the corner of the page. He steadies it with his thumb and continues watching. Intervention would introduce a new variable. He closes the book only after the markers along the line have steadied completely and the orchard resumes its ordinary rhythm — insects working, fence wire tapping lightly in the wind.

---

Rachel remains near the marker Caelen tried to turn, fingers resting briefly against the stone before she lets her hand fall away. The tension there holds steady, though faint memory of compression lingers along the line.

A leaf scrapes across the packed earth near her boot.

Farther down the orchard — beyond where Caelen had stood — another marker gives a brief, uneven tremor. The motion begins and folds back into itself before it can travel.

Rachel’s head lifts.

The trees shift in the wind. Light moves across the trunks in narrow bands. Nothing else stirs.

Behind her, gravel settles under a measured step.

The markers hum evenly.

The orchard remains still long enough to suggest it was always that way.
