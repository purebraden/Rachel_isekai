# Chapter 16 — The Courtyard Test

Morning brings a delegation.

Not formal—no Council banners, no announcement—but deliberate enough that the square empties slightly when they arrive. Three boundary walkers from the western settlements, older than Sivara, carrying the kind of presence that comes from decades of work along unstable lines. They don't ask permission to observe. They simply position themselves near the well with clear sightlines to the marker stones and wait.

Rachel feels their attention like weight.

Vaelis stands with them, book already open. Sivara joins the group briefly—words exchanged too quietly to carry—then steps back toward the house's shadow, arms folded loosely. Not withdrawal. Accounting.

"They came to see for themselves," Arathen says, low enough that only Rachel hears.

"I gathered."

"Vaelis sent word west. They want to know if it's reproducible."

Rachel's stomach tightens. "And if it isn't?"

"Then you're singular." He pauses. "That has consequences too."

The square continues its morning rhythm around them—carts rolling past, voices trading brief greetings, someone calling after a child who's wandered too close to the marker line. Ordinary, except for the cluster of observers who are trying very hard to look like they're not observers.

Rachel crosses toward the eastern markers.

The delegation watches without speaking.

She kneels at the first stone, letting her palms hover above its surface. The boundary hum rises to meet her—steady, familiar, no immediate strain requiring correction. She adjusts her awareness, feeling along the line the way you'd run fingers over a seam to find where the thread has loosened.

There.

Not here, where she's kneeling, but farther—across the square, near the western path where the line curves toward the lower terraces. A small compression, the kind that would normally take an hour or more to manifest as visible distortion.

She doesn't move toward it.

Instead, she lets her focus extend, the way she's learned to widen her vision to catch movement at the periphery. The warmth in her palms spreads—up through her wrists, past her elbows, settling into her shoulders with an intensity she hasn't felt before. Not painful. Just present, like stepping from shade into sunlight.

The compression across the square loosens.

One of the delegation's walkers turns sharply toward the western line.

Rachel remains kneeling at the eastern marker.

The adjustment completes itself without her proximity, tension redistributing through the stones in a shallow ripple that travels the length of the square before settling into the ordinary hum.

She lowers her hands slowly.

Her shoulders still carry the warmth, heat pooling there as if her body has decided this is where it belongs now. She flexes her fingers experimentally. No strain. No exhaustion. Just the odd sensation of having reached across distance without moving.

Behind her, someone exhales audibly.

Vaelis writes.

The eldest of the visiting walkers—a woman with silver streaking her dark hair and a scar running from temple to jaw—approaches the western marker Rachel had corrected from across the square. She kneels, presses both palms flat against the stone, and goes very still.

After a long moment, she rises.

"There's no residual compression," she says, voice carrying more clearly than she probably intends. "The redistribution is complete."

"From seventy paces," another walker adds, and doesn't bother hiding the disbelief.

The square feels thinner suddenly, as if the space itself has become attentive.

Rachel stands, brushing dirt from her knees. Arathen steps closer—not shielding, but present in a way that reminds everyone watching that she's not alone in this.

The silver-haired walker approaches her directly. Up close, her eyes are sharp and assessing, not hostile but not warm either.

"How long have you been training?" she asks.

"A year. A bit more."

The walker glances at Sivara. "Under your instruction?"

"Yes," Sivara replies evenly.

"And you taught distance work."

"I didn't teach that." Sivara's tone doesn't shift. "That emerged on its own."

The walker absorbs this, gaze returning to Rachel. "Can you do it again?"

Rachel's throat tightens. "Probably."

"That's not certainty."

"No," Rachel agrees. "It isn't."

The woman studies her a moment longer, then steps back. The delegation clusters briefly, voices low and urgent. Vaelis remains apart, writing steadily, pen moving across the page with the methodical precision of someone documenting evidence.

Across the square, Caelen has emerged from the house he shares with his father. He's holding his notebook again, watching Rachel with an expression that's equal parts fascination and hunger. When their eyes meet, he doesn't look away.

Mirrel appears behind his son, hand coming to rest on Caelen's shoulder—grounding rather than restraining. But his attention is on Rachel too, and there's something new in it now. Not suspicion. Not fear. Just the careful watchfulness of someone recalculating risk.

The delegation finishes their discussion. The silver-haired walker turns back to Rachel.

"We'll be staying through the week," she says. "To observe."

It's not a request.

"Understood," Sivara says before Rachel can respond.

The walkers disperse slowly, taking up positions around the square's perimeter as if Thornhaven has become territory requiring mapping. Vaelis joins them briefly, then breaks away toward the northern terraces, book tucked under his arm.

Rachel feels the weight of their presence settle over the town like a second boundary—invisible but undeniable.

"This is what happens," Arathen says quietly, "when you become visible."

"I was trying to help."

"I know." His hand brushes her elbow, a brief steadying touch. "That's not always protection."

---

The house is too quiet when they return.

Maelin has tea prepared, which means she saw the delegation arrive and decided anxiety required ritual. Edrin paces near the hearth with loose pages scattered across the table—more archive fragments, notes written in margins too small to read from a distance.

Ilyra sits in the corner, sharpening a blade with slow, deliberate strokes that somehow make the silence louder.

Rachel takes her seat.

"They came from the west," Maelin says, setting down a cup with more force than necessary.

"Three walkers," Rachel confirms. "Vaelis sent word."

"Of course he did." Maelin's voice stays even, but her hands tighten briefly around the teapot. "And?"

"I corrected a strain from across the square."

Edrin stops pacing. "How far?"

"Seventy paces. Maybe more."

"And they witnessed it."

"All of them."

Ilyra's blade stills against the whetstone. "That's not proximity. That's projection."

"I didn't project anything," Rachel says, though the distinction feels flimsy even as she makes it. "I just... extended my awareness."

"To seventy paces." Edrin's tone isn't accusatory, just precise. "That's not standard sensory range."

"I know."

"And the warmth?" Maelin asks, eyes already tracking the way Rachel's rubbing absently at her shoulder.

"Higher. Past my elbows now."

The room absorbs this.

"Structural capacity increasing," Edrin murmurs, half to himself. "If she's redistributing strain at distance, that implies—"

"It implies people will try to replicate it," Arathen interrupts quietly.

Rachel's stomach drops. "Caelen was watching."

"He's always watching," Ilyra says. "That's not new."

"This felt different." Rachel looks at Arathen. "He had that notebook again. Taking notes."

"His father knows," Arathen replies. "Mirrel's not oblivious."

"That's not reassurance," Maelin says sharply. "Mirrel's protective. That doesn't mean he's careful."

Sivara's voice enters from the doorway. "He's both."

They all turn.

Sivara steps inside, closing the door with deliberate care. "Mirrel came to me after the delegation settled. He wants Caelen supervised if he's going to observe Rachel's work."

"Supervised," Rachel repeats slowly. "Not stopped."

"Curiosity doesn't stop," Sivara says, echoing Vaelis's earlier words. "It only moves underground if you try to contain it."

"And if he tries what she does?" Maelin's voice tightens. "At seventy paces? Without her... whatever it is?"

"Then it fails," Sivara says bluntly. "Possibly catastrophically."

The blade scrapes against stone. Ilyra sets it aside.

"So we watch him," she says. "Make sure he doesn't try alone."

"And if others are watching too?" Edrin gestures toward the scattered pages. "The archive fragments mention 'inherited variance'—structural abilities that didn't replicate across populations. Some demons tried. Most failed. A few..." He trails off.

"A few what?" Rachel asks.

Edrin meets her gaze. "A few destabilized themselves trying to force alignment they didn't have."

The silence that follows feels like falling.

"The delegation isn't just here to observe," Sivara says quietly. "They're here to assess risk. Not just yours—everyone's. If your abilities can't be taught, can't be replicated, then you're either an asset or a hazard. They're trying to determine which."

Rachel's throat tightens. "And if they decide hazard?"

"Then the Council gets involved." Sivara's expression doesn't soften. "More than they already have."

Arathen's hand finds Rachel's wrist under the table, thumb pressing gently against her pulse.

"Let them watch," he says. "They'll see what we see. She's not destabilizing anything."

"She's centralizing it," Edrin counters. "That's different. That's—" He stops, searching for words. "That's architectural."

The word sits strangely in the room.

Rachel thinks of the compression dissolving across seventy paces of open air, of the warmth pooling in her shoulders, of the boundary adjusting without her touch.

"I didn't mean to," she says quietly.

"That," Sivara replies, "is what worries them most."

---

Night falls slowly.

Rachel stands at the window, watching the square empty as residents retreat to their houses. The delegation has taken rooms near the eastern edge—close enough to the markers to observe, far enough to avoid being obvious.

Lamplight flickers in Mirrel's house across the way.

Through the window, she can just see Caelen at a table, bent over his notebook, writing with the focused intensity of someone trying to solve a problem through sheer documentation.

Arathen joins her at the window, following her gaze.

"He's going to try," Rachel says.

"Yes."

"And we can't stop him."

"No." Arathen's reflection in the glass is calm, steady. "But we can be there when he does."

The square settles into darkness.

The boundary hum deepens, spreading through the foundation stones, through the packed earth, through the air itself.

Rachel's shoulders still carry the warmth.

Across seventy paces.

Across however much farther it will reach tomorrow.

The delegation is watching.

Caelen is planning.

And the boundary keeps adjusting, keeps recognizing, keeps orienting around her whether she moves or not.

Outside, the markers glow faintly in the moonlight.

Inside, Rachel tries to remember what it felt like when her power had limits she could name.
