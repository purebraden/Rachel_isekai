# Chapter 5 — Eastward Tremor

The ward hum followed Rachel into the hall and refused to smooth itself
back into its usual quiet.

It moved through the stone in uneven currents, tightening and loosening
in places she couldn't see, and each fluctuation brushed against her
awareness just enough to keep her from taking a full breath.

She knocked once on Maelin's door --- firm, deliberate --- and pushed it
open.

"Maelin."

Maelin was upright almost before the word finished leaving Rachel's
mouth, hair falling loose around her shoulders, eyes already focused and
searching.

"You feel it."

Rachel nodded, and as she did another tremor traveled through the floor
beneath them, not violent, but deep enough that the doorframe gave a
faint answering vibration.

"East," she said. "About a day."

Maelin was on her feet immediately, reaching for her outer layers
without looking away from Rachel's face.

"Wake them."

Rachel moved down the corridor, the hum shifting under her feet as if it
were following her stride.

Ilyra's door opened before Rachel could knock, blade already in hand,
posture coiled and alert.

"How far?"

Rachel reached outward again, letting the sensation unfold instead of
forcing it, following the angled pull until it resolved into something
sharper and more distinct than it had been a moment ago.

"A day," she said, though even as she spoke the pressure tightened
further east, as if her attention had drawn it closer.

Ilyra saw something in her expression and didn't press.

Edrin's door stuck as usual; Rachel leaned her shoulder into it, and it
gave way with a dull scrape. He blinked up at her from a sprawl of
parchment and charcoal, sleep clinging to him for half a heartbeat
longer than it had to the others.

"Bring paper," she said, and then, because the thought arrived with a
sudden clarity that felt almost like alarm, "and the east map."

That cut through the last of his drowsiness. He sat upright so quickly
that a loose sheet slid from his lap to the floor, and he reached for
the rolled map pinned above his desk, fingers fumbling only once before
finding purchase.

Arathen met her in the corridor fastening his boots, gaze steady and
already assessing the tension in her shoulders rather than asking for it
in words.

"The town?" he asked.

Rachel let herself listen more deeply, pushing past the surface
vibration of the lattice.

"It's holding," she said, though the word felt thin compared to what she
was sensing. "But something out there is... setting itself."

The house came alive around her in overlapping motion --- leather
creaking as Ilyra tightened straps, Maelin tying off her braid while
pulling on boots in the same fluid movement, Edrin spreading the east
map across the table and leaving faint charcoal smudges as he traced
familiar ridgelines.

"If it's near quarry ridge," Edrin began, tapping lightly, "the stone
there fractures along natural seams. If something's using those
lines---"

The ward hum deepened mid-sentence, not in volume but in weight, and
Rachel felt the shift run through her spine before she understood it.

She stilled.

The tremor didn't dissipate this time. It traveled outward, like a
ripple encountering resistance and refusing to flatten.

"Rachel?" Arathen said quietly.

She closed her eyes, following the hinge eastward again, and this time
the angle felt less distant, less abstract --- it pressed at her
awareness with a clarity that made her breath hitch.

"It's moving faster," she murmured, almost to herself. "It wasn't this
defined when I woke."

Maelin paused near the door, gloves half-pulled on.

"I'll wake Sivara," she said, and there was no question in it.

Rachel hesitated, not because she doubted the decision but because the
hinge flared again as if in answer, tightening another fraction.

"Go," she said, the word catching slightly on the way out.

Maelin slipped into the cold night, the door closing behind her with a
hollow sound that seemed louder than it should have been.

They continued gathering supplies, though the room felt narrower now,
the air charged in a way that made every movement feel consequential.

Ilyra packed dried rations into a satchel without looking down, hands
moving from memory. Arathen retrieved coil-rope and slung it over his
shoulder, then crossed to Rachel and fastened the clasp of her cloak
when her fingers stalled at it for half a second too long.

Edrin rolled the map tightly and bound it with cord, then tucked spare
parchment into his folio with movements that were almost careful enough
to be slow.

Another tremor passed through the lattice.

Stronger.

Rachel moved to the window and pressed her palm briefly against the cool
stone beside it, extending outward again despite the strain it caused
behind her eyes.

The hinge eastward no longer felt like something assembling at a
distance.

It felt like something nearly aligned.

Footsteps approached at last, quick and steady.

The door opened, and Maelin stepped inside, breath misting faintly in
the cold air that followed her.

"She's at the east gate," Maelin said. "Waiting."

That meant Sivara had felt it too.

They moved as one.

The courtyard stones were cool beneath their boots, and as they crossed,
doors along the outer ring opened in quiet succession. Figures stood in
thresholds wrapped in cloaks and blankets, watching the faint ward-lines
carved into the walls glimmer and waver in the dark.

No one spoke.

The tension traveled through the compound like a shared pulse.

They moved through the inner lane at a brisk pace, the training yard
dark to their right, the circle barely visible in low light. Rachel felt
the echo of earlier alignment beneath her feet and had the strange,
disorienting thought that the ground itself remembered being turned.

The hinge flared again.

Sharper now.

By the time the east gate came fully into view, the pressure in her
awareness had intensified enough that she had to steady her breathing
deliberately, as if she were descending too quickly underwater.

Sivara stood beneath the arch, hands clasped behind her back, gaze fixed
beyond the boundary line.

She turned as they approached.

"It is not centered here," Sivara said.

Rachel stepped forward, but the surge that met her almost staggered her
mid-stride.

"It's larger," she said before answering anything else. "It's stronger
than when I woke."

Sivara's eyes narrowed slightly.

"How much?"

Rachel searched for language and found none that captured the
acceleration she was feeling.

"It's nearly locked," she said at last.

The ward hum behind them shifted in answer, a resonance traveling along
the wall like a warning.

"It is forming," Sivara said.

"Yes."

"You hear it clearly."

"I do."

Another pulse struck the hinge eastward --- sudden, decisive --- and
Rachel felt it reverberate through her ribs.

"It's accelerating," she added, unable to keep the urgency from her
voice.

Sivara studied her for a long moment.

"You may misjudge it," she said quietly.

Rachel felt the truth of that and the weight of it together.

"Then I'll correct," she replied, and this time there was no hesitation
in the words.

Sivara stepped closer, lowering her voice.

"If you can sense it changing from this distance, you do not require my
hands."

The transfer landed heavily in the air between them.

"I will hold Thornhaven," Sivara continued. "If the perimeter strains, I
will keep the line intact."

Rachel nodded, though her focus was already pulled eastward again,
toward the hinge that was no longer patient.

They stepped through the gate.

The ward hum altered as they crossed the boundary --- thinning briefly
before sealing behind them --- and ahead, the pull sharpened into
something almost tangible, as if the night itself were angling toward a
decision.

Whatever was setting itself east of Thornhaven was no longer tentative.

It was choosing.

And it was choosing quickly.
