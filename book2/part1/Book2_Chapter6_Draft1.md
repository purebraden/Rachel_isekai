# Chapter 6

The east gate settled shut behind them, and with it the steady
reinforcement of Thornhaven's lattice receded into something distant ---
still present, but no longer carrying her weight.

Outside the boundary, the hinge sharpened immediately.

It pressed through the morning air in a clean, angled line that tugged
somewhere ahead and slightly upward, as though the world itself were
leaning toward a decision still in progress.

They took the quarry road, its surface packed firm from years of carts
and foot traffic. Stone and root braided through the earth beneath their
boots. The trees here rose taller and darker than those nearer town,
bark ridged in spiraling growth that caught pale morning light along its
edges.

For a stretch they walked without speaking.

Rachel let her awareness stretch forward and downward, measuring
distance by pressure instead of sight. The hinge felt closer than it had
at the gate. It carried definition now --- direction and intent layered
together.

Behind her, Maelin cleared her throat softly.

"I bought something yesterday," she said, reaching into her pouch as
they walked.

Ilyra glanced sideways. "You always do."

Maelin ignored the tone and drew out a bundle of braided cords threaded
with flattened river-stones etched in fine ward-lines.

"For travel," she said. "The vendor claimed they ease resistance. I
meant to try them on the next long walk."

She tied one around Rachel's wrist.

The stone lay cool against her skin.

The others followed suit, fastening their charms as they continued
walking.

Within minutes Rachel noticed the difference. Her stride lengthened
without strain, breath settling into a deeper rhythm. The incline ahead
seemed to offer less drag, as though the road met her halfway.

The hinge ahead remained sharp.

Above the treeline to their left, pale-winged gliders rode a thermal
current, membranes flashing silver as they banked. Their clicking calls
stitched the air in steady rhythm.

Mid-arc, the formation buckled.

Two gliders veered sharply. One stalled for the span of a heartbeat
before snapping sideways.

Rachel felt the seam at the same instant --- a thin rotational tension
near the base of the slope ahead.

"Wait a moment," she said, stepping off the road.

The grass trembled faintly. Stone-hoppers froze mid-bound, limbs angled
awkwardly as though gravity had shifted.

She crouched.

Warmth bloomed in her palm --- familiar, contained --- spreading lightly
into her wrist. Not the fierce surge she remembered from tearing free of
the summoning circle, when the heat had climbed high and left her
breathless. This was steadier. Controlled.

She traced the hinge beneath the distortion and rotated it slightly.

The warmth moved halfway up her forearm and settled.

The gliders resumed their pattern overhead. The grass stilled. The
stone-hoppers scattered.

They resumed walking.

After another stretch of road, Edrin slowed slightly and withdrew the
east map from his folio.

The parchment shimmered faintly as he unrolled it, the surface catching
light in subtle lines that only appeared when held at the correct angle.
A pale path traced itself from Thornhaven eastward, branching faintly
along known routes.

He tilted it, watching the faint glow shift.

"It still favors the quarry ridge," he said quietly.

Rachel stepped closer, eyes on the map rather than the road.

The marked route pulsed faintly.

Beneath it, however, she saw something else --- a thinner arc that
curved away from the established line, almost too faint to notice.

"It's bending," she said.

Edrin frowned. "Where?"

She reached toward the map, not touching it, just hovering her fingers
above the surface. The faint secondary line brightened slightly under
her attention.

"There," she said. "It's not following the natural ridge. It's angling
toward this low basin."

Arathen leaned in, but the line faded slightly from his view.

"I can barely see that," he said.

"It's there," Rachel replied.

The hinge pulsed again --- stronger.

The map responded with a faint shimmer, the primary path wavering as
though reconsidering its direction.

Edrin exhaled slowly. "It's updating."

They moved again.

The second fracture struck halfway up the incline.

The distortion rippled across the path ahead, bending the shadow of a
tree into a doubled image. Veil-moths drifting in the shade flickered
abruptly, their translucent wings stuttering as though caught between
frames. One dropped before regaining lift, glow splitting briefly before
settling.

A herd-creature grazing near the treeline lifted its head sharply,
antler-ridges catching light. It stamped once, muscles tightening.

Rachel felt the deeper seam beneath it --- tethered to the main hinge.

"Wait," she said again.

Warmth returned to her palms immediately, stronger now, spreading up
both forearms.

She crouched and followed the structural tension inward, tracing the
seam back toward the central alignment.

It resisted.

The warmth climbed higher along her arms, brushing near her elbows
before easing --- stronger than the first correction, but still far from
the overwhelming strain she had once felt at the west boundary when she
first learned to turn a seam rather than fight it.

She adjusted the base of the hinge more sharply.

The distortion snapped inward.

The air cleared.

The herd-creature lowered its head cautiously.

Rachel remained crouched a moment longer, letting the warmth recede back
into her palms.

"That one's tied to the center," she said.

Edrin rolled the map slightly and checked the faint branching lines. The
secondary arc she had noticed earlier glowed brighter now.

"It's pulling this direction," he murmured.

They walked on.

Hours passed more quickly than they should have. The charms continued to
smooth the effort of climbing, keeping fatigue at the edges of awareness
rather than in the body.

Arathen fell into step beside her.

"You're not straining," he observed.

Rachel flexed her fingers, feeling only residual warmth.

"No."

The hinge ahead pulsed again.

By the time they crested the ridge, the pressure vibrated through her
bones.

Below lay a shallow bowl of exposed stone and sparse grass. At its
center the air bent visibly, light curving along a vertical seam that
pressed upward without yet splitting.

The map in Edrin's hand shimmered faintly, the secondary path now
glowing clearly toward the basin.

Grass leaned inward along a faint arc. Stone along the bowl's edge had
lifted slightly.

The hinge.

Nearly aligned.

Rachel stepped forward slowly, warmth flickering again in her palms even
without contact.

"It's larger than before," she said quietly. "Still accelerating."

She followed the tension inward, beyond the visible distortion.

It carried intent.

Somewhere beyond the seam, something was turning it deliberately.

And it was close to finishing.
