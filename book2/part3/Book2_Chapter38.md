# Chapter 38 — Perimeter

The bell that started in the square never really stopped. It kept getting
picked up by other hands in other streets until the whole town was
ringing.

By the time Rachel and the others reached the command table, the seam
count had doubled and stopped behaving like countable things. New
fractures opened before runners could finish reporting the old ones.

"North wall has three active."

"West spillway splitting."

"South road marker gone unstable."

"Inner lane by the dye house just flashed and vanished."

Edrin stood over the map with charcoal in one hand, marking until his
fingers were black. Every time he thought he'd found the edge of the
pattern, another point lit outside it.

Sivara took control with a voice so steady it cut through panic.

"Primary teams to perimeter. Reserve teams hold inner lines. Nobody casts
alone."

Rethan ignored that last sentence and drove a conventional closure at the
nearest visible seam by the well.

For a breath it looked like it might hold.

Then the seam split around his cast and opened again on both sides,
shallow at first, then deeper.

Rethan stumbled, caught himself, and looked at his own hands with open
disbelief.

Halvare stepped in, tried the same method at reduced force, and got the
same result with cleaner failure.

"It's rejecting compression sequence," he said.

"Everything rejects eventually if you keep pushing wrong," Ilyra snapped,
already moving toward the north line where shouting had turned to
screaming.

The first creature came through ten minutes later, and it came from a
narrow seam behind the tannery wall rather than the deepest fracture,
which should have been trivial and wasn't.
It surfaced low, all angular limbs and pale seam-light in the joints, and
before anyone could regroup two more emerged from opposite sides of town
as if they'd timed their entry to the same unheard signal.

By then seam behavior had changed again.

Some fractures widened and spit sparks of black particulate that burned
through cloth when it landed. Others remained hair-thin and acted like
pressure valves, venting cold that made nearby wards rattle. One near the
southern arch pulsed in regular intervals, each pulse knocking loose dust
from every roof beam in hearing range.

Thornhaven's perimeter stopped being a line and became a field of active
stress.

Arathen sent fighters where he could and pulled civilians back where he
couldn't.

"Keep moving. Don't wait to collect anything. Move."

Maelin and two older shopkeepers turned the meeting hall into a staging
point for children and elders, blankets on the floor, water buckets
against the wall, names shouted and answered until each family had a
count.

Sivara stood in the center of the square and rotated teams through breach
points by voice and memory because the chalk board she'd set up had
already become obsolete.

Edrin's map no longer looked like mapwork. It looked like someone had
thrown a box of charcoal shards across Thornhaven and then drawn lines
between impacts.

"They're not random," he said to no one in particular. "They keep forming
in relation."

Rethan, breathing hard, still tried to impose order through doctrine.

"If we prioritize high-width seams and ignore narrow events—"

"You'll miss the anchors," Halvare cut in, eyes never leaving the nearest
pulsing line. "The narrow ones are carrying structure."

Rethan stared at him as if betrayal had changed uniforms.

Rachel didn't have space for either of them. She moved from point to point
where pressure turned dangerous, correcting what she could, leaving what
she had to, feeling each intervention pull the whole pattern tighter
instead of looser.

At the north line she closed a seam under a collapsing wall and watched
two new fractures open east and west in answer.

At the orchard she turned a hinge out from under a creature's feet and
nearly lost balance when a deeper pulse rolled through her chest from
somewhere far beyond town.

At the west spillway she reached for one seam and felt twelve.

By the time dark settled, Thornhaven's perimeter was ringed in unstable
light.

From above, Edrin said later, it would have looked geometric.

From the street it looked like everyone had too many places to stand and
no place that stayed safe for long.

Council observers who had spent weeks documenting Rachel now stood elbow
to elbow with local walkers trying to keep their casts from feeding the
wrong hinge.

Traditional sealing was not merely struggling. It was being outmaneuvered.

Every method they had relied on for years still worked in isolated
collapses. None of it scaled to what had started tonight.

Near midnight, a runner came in from the outer farms with torn sleeves
and blood on one ear.

"Fractures beyond the boundary road," he gasped. "Not just Thornhaven."

That hit the room like cold water.

Region-level.

Sivara closed her eyes for one second, opened them, and gave the next
order.

"Send warning to the out-hamlets. Pull everybody inside reinforced lines.
Now."

Rachel stood at the western marker post with her hand on stone that had
gone warm under her palm. Beyond the town, seam-light flickered along the
horizon in a shallow arc.

She listened to the pattern through pain and exhaustion and the bells and
the shouting and the creature calls carrying across dark fields.

It was all moving toward something that looked less like chaos and more
like convergence.

She turned back to the square where her people were still fighting to
hold ground that kept changing shape.

"This is the big one," she said to Arathen when he reached her side.

He didn't ask what she meant. He had already seen enough to know.

"Then we hold until morning," he said.

Rachel looked west once more, then toward town center where Sivara was
still directing teams under failing lantern light.

"No," she said quietly. "We hold until it shows us where the center is."
