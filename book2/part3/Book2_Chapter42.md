# Chapter 42 — The Decision

The aperture waited.

It did not pulse wider. It did not threaten. It held at one narrow width
in front of Rachel while the square around her stayed painfully still, as
if the whole town understood that any sudden movement would become part of
the answer.

Sivara stayed at Rachel's left shoulder.

Arathen stayed on her right.

Behind them, the Council and Thornhaven stood in the same ring for once,
all argument postponed by something neither side had language for.

"We can still try closure," Rethan said, and his voice sounded odd in the
open square, too formal for what was in front of him.

Sivara did not turn. "With what cast?"

Rethan had no immediate reply. Meredin answered for him in a tone gentler
than Rachel had ever heard from him.

"Not one we can guarantee."

Rachel lifted her hand toward the opening and felt the structure answer by
settling closer, not physically, but in the way pressure settled when a
load finally found a bearing point.

She could close it, probably.

She could throw every reserve she had into compressing the aperture, force
the line to fold, and spend the next year dealing with whatever those
consequences looked like.

She could stay.

She could keep Thornhaven in sight and pretend that unanswered questions
would stay patient forever.

The resonance under her ribs said otherwise.

It had said otherwise for a long time.

Arathen touched two fingers to the back of her wrist, light enough to
leave her room and solid enough to remind her where she stood.

"Talk to me," he said.

Rachel kept her eyes on the aperture. "If I close this now, we buy time."

"Yes."

"If I don't..."

He gave a short breath. "Then we get the answer tonight instead of waiting
for it to tear through us again."

Sivara's voice came low and precise, the same voice she'd used in training
yards and crisis briefings and the worst hours of this week.

"If you cross, you may not control where you emerge. You may not control
timing. You may not control return."

"I know."

"And if you stay, we can still build a containment strategy around this."

Rachel almost laughed. Not because it was funny, because the sentence was
so exactly Sivara that hearing it in this moment made her chest hurt.

"You taught me not to pretend small choices are safe choices."

Sivara went very still. Then she nodded once.

Around them, Thornhaven began to breathe again in shallow, uneven pulls.
A child in the crowd asked a question too quietly to hear. Maelin
shushed them with a hand at the back of their head and her own eyes fixed
on Rachel.

Edrin clutched his folio to his chest hard enough to bend the corner and
looked as if he wanted to say ten things and knew none of them belonged in
this exact second.

Halvare had stopped writing.

Rethan looked older than he had yesterday.

Meredin had both hands clasped behind his back, gaze lifted not to Rachel
but to the lattice above her, as if he were trying to memorize every line
before it changed.

Rachel took one step closer to the aperture.

The low tone underfoot deepened.

She turned her head to Arathen.

"I have to see."

He did not argue.

He did not ask for one more plan, one more map, one more minute to pretend
they had an option that did not cost everything.

He squeezed her hand once and said, "Then we go."

Sivara's breath caught. "Arathen—"

"I'm not letting her do this alone."

There was no drama in it. It was simply the shape of the man.

Maelin crossed the ring before anyone could stop her and caught Rachel's
face between both hands, thumbs rough from years of work.

"Come back if you can," she said, voice steady by force. "And if you
can't, don't stay where you're not wanted."

Rachel nodded, throat too tight for words.

Maelin stepped away without looking at anyone else.

Sivara put her palm briefly against Rachel's shoulder blade, a blessing or
a command or both.

"Remember your own center."

Rachel stepped into the aperture.

The first sensation was cold without wind.

The second was pressure release, as if a lock had finally clicked in the
direction it had been designed to move.

Then the square was gone.

Arathen came through a heartbeat behind her.

The aperture narrowed immediately after, thinning from doorway to line to
point, then folding out of visible space. The lattice overhead dimmed but
did not vanish entirely; a faint geometry remained in the air above
Thornhaven like chalk left after rain.

In the square, nobody moved for several seconds.

Sivara looked at the place where Rachel had stood, then at Halvare.

"Record this exactly," she said.

Halvare swallowed and opened his ledger with unsteady hands.

Meredin spoke without taking his eyes off the fading line.

"The aperture closed," he said.

Edrin shook his head.

"It recessed," he answered quietly. "That's different."

Sivara turned toward the command platform before the silence could
solidify into despair.

"Reset watches. Full perimeter. We hold this town."

Her voice carried across the square, and people moved because moving was
how you stayed alive.
