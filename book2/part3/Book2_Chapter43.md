# Chapter 43 — Crossing

The first thing Rachel noticed was the light.

It had no source she could point to. It didn't fall from above or flare
from any single direction. It existed, pale and steady, as if brightness
were a property of the air itself.

She stood on a broad surface that looked like stone and felt like
something stricter, smoother, more exact. Lines ran through it in
impossible precision, intersecting at angles that made her shoulders
relax before she understood why.

Arathen came through beside her and caught her elbow, not because she was
falling, because his body had decided for him that first contact mattered.

"You all right?"

Rachel listened to her own breathing. It sounded slower here, stretched at
the edges.

"I think so."

The world around them opened in quiet planes.

Structures rose in the distance, tall and spare, less like towers than
like equations given material form. Bridges crossed empty space in straight
lines that should have looked rigid and somehow didn't, and nowhere in
sight was there a banner, a lit window, or any visible crowd waiting at
the threshold.

Wind moved once across Rachel's face and carried no scent she recognized.

Arathen took one step and then another, testing the ground with the
careful balance of someone who had survived too many uncertain thresholds
to trust any of them on first contact.

"Time feels wrong," he said.

Rachel nodded. "Slow."

His shadow, always a little more active around seams, lay close to him
here, tight and narrow. She could see effort in the set of his jaw as he
adapted.

They were not alone for long.

A figure appeared at the far edge of the nearest structure and started
toward them.

At first it looked like distance was tricking her. The movement was so
measured it barely read as movement at all. Then she realized this was
what motion looked like in a place where time had a different grip.

The figure was male, taller than Arathen by a little, dressed in plain,
structured fabric that caught the sourceless light and held it at the
edges. He carried no weapon. His hands were visible. His pace never
changed.

Rachel felt recognition before she had any reason for it, and it came by
resonance rather than face or memory.

The same harmonic line she had been chasing through seams and dreams and
town walls was here in him, clear and undeniable, as intimate as pulse.

Arathen shifted half a step forward, protective instinct outpacing logic.
Rachel touched his wrist.

"Wait."

He waited.

When the stranger reached speaking distance, he stopped and looked at
Rachel as if every expectation he'd carried had collided with a reality he
did not have language for.

His eyes moved over her face once, then to Arathen, then back.

Rachel saw hope arrive first.

Then confusion.

Then something raw enough that she almost looked away.

He took in a breath that sounded like someone trying not to break a
decades-long habit of composure in front of strangers.

When he spoke, his voice was low and roughened by held-back urgency.

"Where is she?"

Rachel stared at him.

She didn't need him to say the name. The question itself carried it.

Theryn.

From his expression, from the weight in the words, from the way his eyes
kept searching her face as if she might change into someone else if he
looked long enough, she understood too many things at once.

He didn't know about her.

He had not been waiting for a daughter.

He had been waiting for the woman who had left and never returned.

For him, that waiting had not stretched into decades.

It had stretched into something shorter and sharper and still unfinished.

Arathen looked from Rachel to the stranger and then back, and Rachel felt
his hand close around hers without pressure, a silent anchor while the
ground shifted under meaning.

The stranger took one careful step closer, not threatening, just unable to
hold his distance anymore.

"Please," he said, and that one word carried hope, fear, grief, and a love
so old it had worn through every defense.

Rachel opened her mouth.

Nothing came.

There were a hundred true things she could have said and none of them
would land cleanly in this first moment.

I don't know where she is.

I am hers.

I crossed a world to get here.

You are too late and maybe so am I.

The light around them did not change. The structures in the distance did
not move. Time held its slow, patient shape while Rachel stood between her
mother's absence and her father's hope and found no language that felt
merciful enough.

She kept his gaze and let the silence answer first.
